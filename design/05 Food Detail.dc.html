When can babies have banana?

From about 6 months, as soon as solids start — no cooking needed, which makes banana the ultimate diaper-bag first food. Ripeness is the whole recipe: yellow with brown speckles means sweet and gum-mashable; green shoulders mean starchy, firm, and constipating.

How do I serve it at each age?

6–8 MO 9–12 MO 12–24 MO

A ripe banana snapped in half with the bottom half of the peel left on — a two-finger length of fruit above a non-slip peel handle.

PASS / FAIL

The ripeness press: a fingertip should dent the fruit easily, no green firmness. Then grip the peel handle yourself — the fruit shouldn't slide out of it.

Why this form: peeled banana shoots out of a small fist like a bar of wet soap; the peel gives a palmar-grasping baby a grippy, food-safe handle.

How to prepare

01

Choose a ripe banana — yellow with brown speckles, no green shoulders.

02

Snap or cut it in half crosswise, keeping the peel on the serving half.

03

Peel the top half down and trim level, leaving two fingers of fruit above the peel base.

04

Hand it over peel-end down and let the baby gnaw the exposed fruit.

Common mistakes

✕

Serving a fully peeled chunk that squirts out of the fist and frustrates the baby.

✕

Using an underripe banana — firm, starchy fruit is harder to mash on gums and harsher on digestion.

How much?

Half a banana in its peel handle — most babies gnaw off far less than the half, and that's exactly right.

Amounts are starting points, not targets — watch the baby, not the numbers.

Is banana a choking hazard?

Low risk when ripe and served in the right form — the peel-handle and pinky-nail-piece shapes above do the safety work. Skip round coin slices at 9–12 months: they're slippery and a less safe shape than irregular segment pieces. If pieces slip away, dust them with dry infant oat cereal or finely ground peanut for grip.

Anything to watch for?

If stools slow down, banana is a usual suspect — ease off for a few days and lean on pears or prunes instead.
